Beam time is expensive and finite. Push the flux too high and the device spends the campaign recovering from functional interrupts instead of producing verifiable results; push it too low and you pay for beam you barely use. Enter six measurable parameters below to find where you sit and what to change.

Reading the results

Interrupts per work cycle, x

The single most useful diagnostic: how many interrupts you expect during one attempt at producing a result. Dimensionless, with a natural target near 0.2 to 0.5.

Value of xMeaningWhat to do
below 0.05Under-driven. Almost every cycle completes, but you are buying more beam time than you need.Raise flux, or lengthen the work cycle.
0.05 to 1.0Well matched. You lose some cycles, but the throughput gain outweighs the loss.Fine tune toward u*.
above 1.0Over-driven. Most cycles are destroyed before they finish and the campaign becomes a recovery loop.Reduce flux, shorten the cycle, or make recovery cheaper.

Throughput relative to achievable, ηrel

The percentage of the best possible data rate you currently get, holding everything fixed except flux. Above 90% means flux is not your bottleneck and effort belongs elsewhere. Below 50% means flux is genuinely mis-set and correcting it is the cheapest improvement available.

What flux cannot fix

Optimal flux only rescues the third factor. The other two are design choices: raise f, since every extra percent of duty cycle in the circuit of interest is an equal percent more data with no interrupt penalty, and lower R, since recovery cost both reduces throughput and drags the optimal flux downward.

Symbol reference

Inputs you supply

SymbolNameUnitsMeaning and how to measure it
\(\phi\)Fluxions cm−2 s−1 Particle rate arriving at the device surface. Read from facility dosimetry.
\(\sigma_{\text{SEFI}}\)SEFI cross sectioncm2 Device-level susceptibility to a functional interrupt: the effective target area a single ion must hit to interrupt the device. Count interrupts, divide by delivered fluence. Varies with LET.
\(\sigma_{\text{circ}}\)Observable cross sectioncm2 Same idea for the specific effect you are measuring in the circuit of interest, such as a corrupted result. Sets how many events you collect. Does not shift the optimal flux.
\(W\)Work cycle durations One complete compute-and-report iteration. An interrupt anywhere inside it invalidates the whole cycle. Mean of your logged per-iteration times.
\(f\)Targeting efficiencydimensionless, 0 to 1 Fraction of the work cycle spent exercising the circuit of interest. From per-iteration timing: compute time divided by total iteration time.
\(R\)Nominal recovery costs Mean time to recover from one interrupt, beyond the destroyed cycle, absent further interrupts.
\(N\)Target event countevents How many confirmed observations you need for adequate statistics.

Quantities the tool derives

SymbolNameUnitsDefinition and how to read it
\(\lambda\)Interrupt rates−1 \(\lambda=\phi\,\sigma_{\text{SEFI}}\). Interrupts per second at this flux.
\(x\)Interrupts per work cycledimensionless \(x=\phi\,\sigma_{\text{SEFI}}\,W=\lambda W\). The main diagnostic; target roughly 0.2 to 0.5.
\(R_{\text{eff}}\)Effective recovery times \(R_{\text{eff}}=(e^{\lambda R}-1)/\lambda\) when the beam stays on during recovery, because a further interrupt restarts it. Equals \(R\) only if recovery is protected. Grows very fast with flux.
\(\rho\)Recovery cost ratiodimensionless \(\rho=R_{\text{eff}}/W\). How many work cycles' worth of time one interrupt costs.
\(\eta\)Observation efficiencydimensionless, 0 to 1 \(\eta=e^{-x}/[1+\rho(1-e^{-x})]\). Fraction of the ideal data rate surviving interrupts and recovery. \(\eta=1\) would mean no interrupts at all.
\(\dot{\Phi}_{\text{eff}}\)Effective fluence rate ions cm−2 s−1 \(\dot{\Phi}_{\text{eff}}=\phi f\eta\). The central output. Of all the fluence you are paying for, the part that actually lands on the circuit of interest during a cycle that completes and is verified. Everything else is wasted: delivered while the wrong block was active, or during a cycle later destroyed. The ratio \(\dot{\Phi}_{\text{eff}}/\phi=f\eta\) is your overall beam-time utilisation.
\(u^{*}\)Optimal interrupts per cycledimensionless Value of \(x\) maximising throughput at a given \(\rho\). Solves \((1-u^{*})(1+\rho)=\rho e^{-u^{*}}\).
\(\phi_{\text{opt}}\)Optimal fluxions cm−2 s−1 \(\phi_{\text{opt}}=u^{*}/(\sigma_{\text{SEFI}}W)\) for protected recovery; numeric when exposed.
\(\eta_{\text{rel}}\)Throughput vs achievabledimensionless, 0 to 1 Current data rate divided by the rate at \(\phi_{\text{opt}}\). Above 90% means flux is not the bottleneck.

Event rate follows by multiplying the effective fluence rate by the observable cross section: \(\text{events per second}=\dot{\Phi}_{\text{eff}}\,\sigma_{\text{circ}}\). Units check: \(\mathrm{cm^{-2}s^{-1}}\times\mathrm{cm^{2}}=\mathrm{s^{-1}}\).
