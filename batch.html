Flux is not the only way to change \(x\). Batching several results before reporting them lengthens the work cycle, and \(x=\phi\sigma_{\text{SEFI}}W\) grows with it. Because batching helps and hurts at the same time, an optimum exists.

Why an optimum exists

  • Larger batches raise \(f\). Fixed per-transfer overhead is amortised over more results, so a greater share of the cycle is spent computing rather than talking. This is pure gain.
  • Larger batches raise \(x\). The vulnerable window grows in proportion, so a single interrupt destroys more work. Past \(x\approx1\) most batches never complete at all.

The first effect saturates: once fixed overhead is negligible, \(f\) stops improving. The second does not. So yield rises quickly, flattens, then falls away.

$$W(B)=t_0+B\,t_s,\qquad f(B)=\frac{B\,w_c}{W(B)}$$

Flux, \(\sigma_{\text{SEFI}}\), \(\sigma_{\text{circ}}\) and \(R\) are shared with the calculator; changing them on either page keeps them in step.

Across batch sizes

Optimal batch size for a chosen recovery time

The explorer above lets you shape the batch-cost model by hand. This second check instead uses the work cycle measured directly on the campaign's FRAM batch sweep, fitted from the runs at \(B=50\) and \(B=200\):

$$W(B) = 13.35 + 4.053\,B \quad \text{ms}$$

A longer batch is more likely to be interrupted, and under an exposed recovery the recovery that follows is itself more likely to be interrupted, so raising \(B\) is penalised twice. Reading it

The curve is steep on the left and gentle on the right, so under-batching costs more than mild over-batching, up to the point where \(x\) approaches 1. Reporting every result individually is rarely optimal unless per-transfer overhead is negligible or interrupts are frequent.

Two practical notes. First, \(B^{*}\) moves with flux: raising flux shortens the batch you can afford, because the vulnerable window has to shrink to keep \(x\) in range. Second, if even the best batch size leaves \(x>1\), batching cannot save you and flux must come down first.

In the campaign on the validation page, the batch-200 builds sat at \(x\approx2\) with 11–15% of batches surviving, and produced two verified computation errors between them against 28 for the batch-1 build.
